▲ Required training · OHSA

Workplace violence & harassment — know it, refuse it, report it

Workplace violence is any use — or threat — of physical force that could injure you: a shove, a raised fist, a thrown object, or a threat that makes you fear for your safety. Harassment is a course of comments or conduct someone knows, or ought to know, is unwelcome — including sexual harassment. Both are covered by Ontario's Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA).

As a driver you're often alone and off-site — at customer docks, truck stops, and border crossings. Aggression can come from people outside CPC as much as inside it. This training is about spotting it early, keeping yourself safe, and knowing that reporting is safe too.

What it can look like
  • An aggressive customer or member of the public at a delivery
  • Threats, intimidation, or someone blocking you in
  • Repeated unwelcome comments about your appearance, race, or gender
  • Bullying or humiliation from a coworker or supervisor
Your rights under OHSA
  • A workplace with a violence & harassment program
  • Information and instruction on that program (this module)
  • The right to refuse unsafe work
  • No reprisal for reporting in good faith

How to report: get to safety first, then call dispatch or your supervisor as soon as you can — you can also report in writing. CPC keeps it confidential, investigates, and takes action. You're protected from any penalty for raising a genuine concern.

Knowledge check · answer all three

1 · Someone at a delivery site turns aggressive and threatening. What's your first move?
That's it. Your safety comes first — disengage when you safely can, then report so CPC can act.
2 · Which of these is workplace harassment?
Correct. Reasonable, work-related direction isn't harassment — repeated unwelcome personal comments are.
3 · You report a safety concern in good faith. What can CPC do?
Right. Reprisal is prohibited. A good-faith report is always handled confidentially and taken seriously.
